WHAT TO DRAW: 8 FRAMES OF A RUN CYCLE

The character runs to the RIGHT. This is a LOOP - frame 8 leads back into frame 1.

*** THE MOST IMPORTANT THING: STRIDE WIDTH ***
Running is shown by how far apart the two feet are, NOT by bouncing up and down.
The number below each frame is the horizontal distance between the two feet,
measured as a percentage of the character's total body height.

FRAME 1   stride 13%   PASSING. Rear leg swinging forward, passing the standing
                       leg. Feet nearly together.
FRAME 2   stride 31%   REACHING. Front leg extending forward, knee bent.
FRAME 3   stride 50%   CONTACT. WIDEST STRIDE. Front heel striking the ground,
                       rear leg stretched far behind. This is the key pose.
FRAME 4   stride 18%   PUSH OFF. Rear foot pushing off, body driving forward.

FRAMES 5-8 repeat frames 1-4 EXACTLY, but with the LEFT and RIGHT legs SWAPPED,
and the arms swapped to match. Same stride widths: 13%, 31%, 50%, 18%.

ARMS - swing them, this is what sells the run
- The arms are bent about 90 degrees at the elbow.
- The arms swing OPPOSITE to the legs. When the RIGHT leg is forward, the LEFT
  arm is forward and the right arm is back.
- The arm swing must be clearly visible and clearly different in every frame.
- Hands are empty (no bow), so nothing blocks the swing.

UPPER BODY
- The torso leans FORWARD in every frame. The head sits about 10% of body height
  ahead of the hips. He is always leaning into the run.
- The shoulders rotate slightly opposite to the hips.

VERTICAL MOVEMENT - keep it small
- The head rises and falls by no more than 3% of body height across the cycle.
- At least one foot stays at or very near the ground in every frame.
- DO NOT make him jump. A previous attempt looked like small hops instead of
  running, and that was wrong. The legs travel HORIZONTALLY, not vertically.

WHAT A GOOD RESULT LOOKS LIKE
Laid side by side, the 8 frames should show the legs opening and closing twice,
the arms pumping opposite to them, and the same character throughout.

DO NOT DRAW ANY OF THESE
- The bow. Hands stay empty.
- A long cloak or robe covering the legs. The legs must stay visible in every
  frame - this is why this character was chosen.
- A ground line, a shadow, or a horizon under the feet.
- Frame numbers, labels, borders, or a grid.
- Smoke, mist, aura, or glow rings. These are drawn by code later.
- A different character. Every frame must be the SAME person as the reference.
- Front view, back view, or three-quarter view.
- Text, watermark, signature.
